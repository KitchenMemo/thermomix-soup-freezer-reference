Lookup table for 26 soups: what to freeze as is, what to hold back, and how to fix it after thawing. Plus storage limits, reheating temperatures and a troubleshooting grid.

Quick Reference
  • Best quality window: 2–3 months at 0°F (−18°C). Continuously frozen soup stays safe longer, but quality declines.
  • Before freezing: refrigerate cooked soup 3–4 days max (USDA) or 48 hours (UK FSA). Day 5 means discard, not freeze.
  • Cooling: no more than 2 hours at room temperature. Use shallow containers.
  • Reheat to: 165°F (74°C) throughout; bring soups to a rolling boil.
  • Rule of thumb: starch (potato, rice, pasta) and dairy/coconut milk are the texture risks. Freeze the base, finish after thawing.

Freeze-the-base method

  1. Cook the soup, stopping before delicate finishing ingredients.
  2. Move to shallow containers and cool promptly.
  3. Refrigerate, then freeze in labelled, dated portions with headspace.
  4. Thaw safely (fridge overnight is predictable); frozen soup can also be reheated in a saucepan or microwave.
  5. Add cream, coconut milk, cooked pasta or rice, or fresh herbs.
  6. Reheat to 165°F (74°C); blend briefly if a smooth soup needs it, following your model’s hot-liquid instructions.
